## 0. Two headline "not in source" findings

1. **D5.4 has no targets framework.** Despite the task name "Governance, Measures & Targets", D5.4 sets no governance or ecological targets. "Target(s)" appears only as references to external targets: EU Nature Restoration Regulation binding restoration targets (D54:552), Kunming-Montreal 30x30 (D54:3117), MSFD GES descriptors D1/D6 and Art. 13 PoMs for *P. oceanica* (D54:6040-6059), Italian Biodiversity Strategy 2030 (30% MPAs, 1/3 strictly protected; D54:6071-6079), national/regional climate targets via seagrass blue carbon (D54:1920, 6657). The conclusion explicitly defers "case specific indicators to first measure, and eventually track, progress for governance" to further research (D54:2274-2279). The only indicator-elicitation instrument is Annex 6 Section 4 (D54:7307-7497): 26 topics rated 1-5 importance with a free-text "How can we measure progress/performance?" column. The DA characterisations carry only vague "Indicator/Target" fields (TUS-CH:143 "Identification of measures to promote sustainable tourism"; JOINT:287 Macaronesia "develop a plan for an effective MPA corridor").
2. **The EBM framework & implementation guidance contains no step list.** It is a *typology* (policy instruments × management measures) plus an evaluation rubric (Barnard & Elliott 2015 "10 tenets" with minimal/full compliance definitions) (EBMF:1-265). There is no scoping→ISA→CLD→analysis→response sequence to map one-to-one; mapping onto the tool's workflow must be done by us (proposal in §3.5).

## 1. D5.4 analytical framework

Three interrelated components (D54:684-690; 1079-1089): **(1) Governance Arrangements (GAs)** structure the policy context; **(2) New Public Governance (NPG)** is the "lens" for assessing how the GA performs; **(3) EBM principles** are the normative reference point against which recommendations are made. Research question + 3 sub-questions (D54:587-599): what governance elements enable/hinder EBM; what capacities (human, institutional, financial) are needed and where the gaps are; what cross-sectoral/transboundary cooperation opportunities exist.

### 1.1 Governance Arrangement components (Table 1, D54:721-751; after Leroy & Arts 2006, D54:629-632)

| Code (used in P2025) | Component | Definition (abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| GA-A | **Actors** | Public and private actors, coalitions, organisations, agencies; local to international governments, industry, civil society (D54:724-726). **Actor-relationship typology: inputters, extractors, regulators, beneficiaries, affected, influencers** — roles may overlap (D54:727-733). |
| — | **Discourses** | How actors define problems, norms, values and solutions (D54:734-739). |
| RoG | **Rules of the game** | Formal rules/procedures across the policy cycle stages *agenda setting, policy formulation, decision making, implementation, evaluation*, plus informal rules; includes enforcement responsibilities (D54:741-744). Steering mechanisms = law, (in)formal regulation, compliance mechanisms (D54:715-718). |
| Re | **Power (resources)** | Unequal distribution of money, information, knowledge, expertise, formal responsibilities. Three forms: **relational, dispositional, structural** (D54:745-751). |

Additional GA-level descriptors used in every DA annex (headings): Actors; Rules of the game & steering mechanisms; Discourses; Power; Effectiveness of current regulations; Challenges and gaps (Arctic D54:3023-3331; Macaronesia D54:4207-4900; Tuscan D54:5845-6362). Legislative structure is visualised per country as **organograms** (institutions) and **"horrendograms"** (international→regional→national legislation; Boyes & Elliott 2014) — Figs 2-8, 11-17, 19-21 (D54:181-207). These are images; not extractable from the text.

Further D5.4 challenge typology for ocean governance (Exec. summary, D54:445-460): (i) fragmented responses / unclear mandates; (ii) competing interests for space/resources; (iii) difficulty to zone/partition; (iv) power imbalances & limited cross-sector collaboration; (v) limited data/capacity; (vi) climate change unpredictability; (vii) technology outpacing governance; (viii) geopolitics.

### 2.5 Cross-cutting conclusions (for narrative/guidance text)
- Advancing EBM depends less on new instruments than on coordinating existing GAs across sectors, levels and actors (D54:1967-1978).
- Persistent challenges: trust, overlapping mandates, siloed ministries, historical grievances (D54:2265-2267).
- NPG paper policy implications (NPG:361-369): collective knowledge arrangements/co-creation; horizontal integration (multi-level cross-sectoral EBM strategies); vertical integration (informal or official links local→global); address power asymmetries (inclusion, transparency, capacity); mechanisms for long-standing grievances (participatory enabling conditions, distributional mechanisms/benefit sharing, conflict resolution).
- Discussion themes (NPG:339-357): co-produced knowledge creates public value; presenting outcomes is not sufficient; unaddressed power imbalances undermine public value; fragmented structures hinder integrated management — "clear identification of competent authorities (legal mandate) distinct from coordinating bodies" (NPG:353); collaboration alone does not guarantee public value.

## 3. EBM framework & implementation guidance (EBMF / EBMF53)

### 3.1 Key definitions (only in the Task 5.3 variant, EBMF53:5-13)
- **Management strategy** = policy instrument + management measure (the instrument is the means to implement the measure).
- **Management plan** = internally consistent combination of measures and instruments aimed at selected policy objectives for a specific ecosystem and its socio-economic/institutional context.
- **Management measures** operate solely in the **ecological system** (target stressors/receptors; mitigate pressures or enhance ecosystem components).
- **Policy instruments** operate solely in the **social system** (target actors' behaviour).
This is the conceptual hinge for the tool: **Measures ↔ DAPSIWRM P/C nodes; Instruments ↔ actors (D/A, stakeholders)**; both sit under "R/M" and governance arrangements determine instrument feasibility (EBMF:1).

### 3.2 Policy instrument typology (EBMF:10-93; after UNEP 2007)
1 **Legal & regulatory**: 1.1 Standards/limits/requirements; 1.2 Supervision/enforcement; 1.3 Treaties; 1.4 Targets/policy development; 1.5 Monitoring systems/supervision; 1.6 Bans
2 **Voluntary agreements & information**: 2.1 Codes of conduct; 2.2 Certification schemes; 2.3 Awareness campaigns/education; 2.4 Labelling; 2.5 Nudging
3 **Economic incentive-based (incl. market-based)**: 3.1 Subsidies & grants; 3.2 Tariffs; 3.3 Deposit-refund; 3.4 Taxes (relief) & charges; 3.6 Liability schemes; 3.7 ITQ/cap-and-trade (note: 3.5 missing in source numbering)
4 **Research & development (knowledge base)**: 4.1 Research (monitoring/investments/programmes); 4.2 Impact assessments; 4.3 Evaluation

### 3.3 Management measure typology (EBMF:105-168)
| Category | Definition | Types / examples |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial distribution control | Mitigate spatial extent of activities/pressures | Year-round restriction (strict MPA, no-take, OECMs, real-time closures); Seasonal closure/restriction |
| Input control | Mitigate the activity | Capacity (decommissioning); Effort (days-at-sea) |
| Output control | Mitigate the pressure | TAC/quota, littering ban |
| Remediation | Reduce pressure from past activities | Fishing for Litter, beach cleaning, ghost gear retrieval |
| Restoration | Enhance ecosystem component state | Species/stock restoration; Habitat restoration (greening grey infrastructure) |
| Nature-based solutions | Novel activities enhancing ecosystem state | Artificial reefs |
| Multi-use | Co-existence/co-location/symbiotic use | Replace existing activities (low-trophic aquaculture, wind-powered shipping) |

### 3.4 10-tenets evaluation rubric (EBMF:182-265; Barnard & Elliott 2015)
Applied to the **measure** (3): Ecologically effective (minimal / **intermediate** / full), Technologically feasible, Economically efficient.
Applied to the **whole intervention** (instrument + measure) (8): Economically viable, Socially desirable/tolerable, Ethically defensible, Culturally inclusive, Legally permissible, Administratively achievable, Effectively communicable, Politically expedient. (That is 3 + 8 = 11 rows; the 10 classic Barnard & Elliott tenets are all present and "Economically efficient" is an added measure-level criterion. Also the "Administratively achievable — full compliance" definition contains a copy-paste error: "cannot ascertain" appears in the full-compliance text, EBMF:255.)
Each tenet has normative **minimal vs full** compliance definitions (only ecological effectiveness has an intermediate level).

**Governance-relevant tenets**: Legally permissible (statutes exist to enable/enforce), Administratively achievable (competent bodies with track record), Politically expedient, Effectively communicable — these map directly to D5.4 findings (overlapping mandates, enforcement capacity, transparency).

### 4.3 Interview protocols (qualitative, not tool-hostable as-is)
- Annex 4 general protocol (D54:6847-6964): Q1 About you; Q2 GA (General, Actors, Rules of the game/steering/effectiveness, Discourses); Q3 Decision-making split **before** (trust), **during** (value centred, equitable outcomes, cross-sectoral, participation, legitimacy), **after** (transparency, communication, compliance/enforcement); Q4 Resources & capacity (who controls access); Q5 Recommendations. Each question is tagged with its NPG construct in parentheses (D54:6920-6948) — directly reusable as a checklist.
- P2025 (Arctic, Aug 2025): items coded **GA-A** (actors/cooperation/power/trust/inclusion), **RoG** (policies, science-catch mismatch, needed changes), **Re** (resources), **NPG** (positive/negative collaboration cases), **Con** (transparency, conflict resolution, "top three measures") (P2025:15-49). Ready-made coding scheme for free-text fields.
- Annex 6 (Arctic 2023-24, D54:7213-7504): dependency on 7 countries rated 1-5; **26 topics rated 1-5 importance + "how can we measure progress"** (biodiversity conservation, climate shifts in distribution, emerging species, species interactions, spatial management, quota allocation, ecolabelling, carbon footprint, local sourcing, circular economy, species disappearing, green financing, energy transition at sea/on land, product innovation, climate impacts on infrastructure, conflicts with other industries, CSR, sustainability reporting, inclusion & transparency in political decision-making, noise, air pollution, integrating industries, labour, cultural heritage, social network).

**Can the tool host a governance self-assessment / EBM readiness check?** Yes. The 7-option agree scale + Yes/No/Sometimes items are directly form-compatible; scoring can aggregate per NPG dimension (e.g. agree=+2…disagree=−2, "don't know"/"prefer not" = NA). Caveat: D5.4 used these instruments descriptively (no scoring, no thresholds, no validated index) — any readiness score is a toolbox construct and must be labelled as such. Ethics: the tool must not store identifiable answers without consent (the project used GDPR consent forms; there is a `MarineSABRES_Infomed_consent_GDPR_RIFS.md`).
